02 In the headset

Never take the headset off

Double-tap the menu button and the settings panel appears inside VR — refresh rate, bitrate, codec, sharpening, buffering — applied with the A button while the game keeps running. Both thumbsticks summon a full performance HUD; right stick plus A captures the frame you're seeing.

03 Overlays

Panels that live in the void

Race dashboards, knee boards and HUDs aren't baked into the picture — each one is routed as its own layer. Position it in the black space around the game, stick it to your visor, hide it, or pull it back into the world. Per-panel control, from the PC or the headset.

Per-panel control

Every overlay gets its own identity — stick it to the visor, release it, hide it, show it, or drop it back into the game.

Metric placement

Pin any panel at exact coordinates in view space — the black above the cockpit is usable real estate.

Uncropped by design

04 Desktop mode

Your whole PC, floating in the void

Not every session is a game. Desktop mode streams your primary monitor onto a panel over passthrough — aim with the right controller, trigger clicks, A right-clicks, the stick scrolls. It yields automatically the moment a game claims the stream. Experimental, and already useful.

Over passthrough

The panel floats in your room, not inside a game — check a browser, a dashboard or a chat without leaving VR.

Yields to games

Launch a title and desktop mode steps aside automatically; exit and it's waiting again.

Honest status

06 Connection

It finds the headset. You just play.

No IP addresses, no pairing rituals. The Streamer discovers the headset automatically, keeps the session crash-safe, and can flip the whole system between VLXR and your other runtime with one button — the headset cold-launches and comes back ready.

Auto-discovery

Automatic discovery finds the headset on your network and trusts it — no manual IP entry, ever.

Crash-safe session

Session state is written atomically with last-good restore; a killed streamer doesn't strand the headset.

One-click runtime switch

Switch to VLXR or back — ordered PC-side-first, runtime flip, headset cold-launch, verified link. Tray goes green when the headset is ready.

08 Questions

The honest answers

When does it release?

It's in late development on real hardware. This page exists so the shape of the product is public before the binaries are — follow the project page for the announcement.

Which headsets are supported?

Meta Quest headsets on PC — the console and headset sides are developed and tested together as one matched pair.

Will it be free?

That's a decision for release day, not a promise made today. The project page will carry the real terms.

What do I need to run it?

A Windows PC, a supported headset on the same network, and a GPU with a hardware video encoder. The Streamer app walks you through the rest.

Why is it closed source?

Streaming runtimes are a cat-and-mouse space. VLXR's pacing, capture and control surfaces are the product — they'll stay private while the user experience stays open.

Can I try it now?

Not yet — there's no public build. The interactive console on this page is a labelled illustration of the real controls.
